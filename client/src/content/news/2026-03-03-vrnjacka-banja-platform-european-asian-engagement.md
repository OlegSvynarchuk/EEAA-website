Vrnjačka Banja continues to position itself as more than a traditional spa destination, emerging instead as a testing ground for broader forms of European-Asian cooperation. During a visit organised with the support of the European Economic Alliance for Cooperation with Asia (EEAA), discussions brought together representatives of local institutions, tourism stakeholders and the business community to explore concrete avenues for future collaboration.

Talks covered a wide range of themes, from medical, wellness and spa tourism to digitalisation, service innovation and investment potential. Representatives of the Vrnjačka Banja Tourist Organisation and local travel agencies outlined the destination's capacity to respond to changing global demand, particularly from Asian markets where interest in integrated healthcare and wellness services continues to grow.

Healthcare institutions also played a central role in the discussions, with the Merkur Special Hospital highlighting opportunities for international cooperation in rehabilitation, preventive medicine and long-term treatment programmes. These strengths were discussed not only as tourism assets, but as part of a wider healthcare and knowledge-based offer.

Local entrepreneurs and business representatives contributed to the dialogue by presenting opportunities in hospitality, services and supporting industries, underlining the importance of aligning public initiatives with private-sector readiness. Digitalisation was identified as a cross-cutting theme, seen as essential for improving accessibility, visibility and operational efficiency in engaging international partners.

The visit reinforced the idea that effective European-Asian cooperation increasingly depends on local ecosystems rather than centralised strategies. In this context, Vrnjačka Banja was presented as a place where tourism, healthcare, business and institutions intersect - offering a practical model for how smaller European destinations can engage credibly with Asia's evolving economic landscape.
