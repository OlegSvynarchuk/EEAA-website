As Asia's economic centres continue to expand their global reach, Europe is being observed with renewed interest - not only for its markets, but for its ability to articulate a coherent offer. It was against this broader backdrop that Teodor Kern, representative of the European Economic Alliance for Cooperation with Asia (EEAA), met with Vladimir Kokanović, Director of the Administration for Cooperation with the Diaspora at Serbia's Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

The discussion focused on how Serbia might position itself more clearly across Asian markets at a time when economic partnerships are increasingly shaped by credibility, continuity and local knowledge. Particular attention was given to the Serbian diaspora, viewed not simply as a cultural community abroad but as a strategic network capable of linking institutions, businesses and investors across regions.

In a world where Asian economies are diversifying their partnerships beyond traditional Western hubs, diaspora ties can function as informal infrastructure - thus reducing risk, accelerating trust and opening channels that formal diplomacy alone often cannot. The meeting explored how these networks could be more systematically integrated into Serbia's international economic outreach.

Regional cooperation also featured prominently in the talks. Rather than approaching Asia as individual national actors, South-East and Eastern Europe were discussed as a collective platform with greater visibility and relevance. In this context, cooperation between public institutions and international economic organisations such as EEAA was seen as essential.

The meeting reflected a shared understanding that Asia is not merely watching Europe, but assessing its readiness. For Serbia, the challenge - and opportunity - lies in turning proximity, networks and regional alignment into a coherent and credible proposition.
