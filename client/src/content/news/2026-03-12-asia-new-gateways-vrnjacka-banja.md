As Asia's economic interest in Europe increasingly extends beyond traditional capitals, smaller cities and regions are finding themselves unexpectedly in focus. It was in this context that Teodor Kern, representative of the European Economic Alliance for Cooperation with Asia (EEAA), met this week with Boban Đurović, Mayor of Vrnjačka Banja, to explore opportunities for deeper European-Asian cooperation, with Eastern Europe positioned as both bridge and beneficiary.

The discussions centred on the promotion of Vrnjačka Banja's long-established role in rehabilitation, medical and spa tourism - sectors that are gaining renewed relevance as Asian markets show growing interest in preventive healthcare, wellness and long-term recovery programmes. With its natural resources, medical expertise and mature tourism infrastructure, the spa town was presented not merely as a destination, but as a platform for sustainable investment.

Beyond tourism, the meeting addressed wider investment opportunities, including healthcare infrastructure, hospitality and related services, alongside possibilities for institutional partnerships and knowledge exchange. Particular attention was given to potential cooperation with Shanghai, one of Asia's most influential economic and innovation hubs, signalling an ambition to connect local capacities with global networks.

Both sides emphasised that municipal and regional cooperation can play a decisive role in strengthening European-Asian ties, especially in sectors where local specialisation aligns with global demand. Rather than relying on abstract strategies, the focus was placed on practical steps - from identifying priority projects to establishing channels for continued institutional engagement.

The meeting in Vrnjačka Banja reflects EEAA's broader approach: grounding European-Asian cooperation in specific places, concrete sectors and realistic timelines. As Asia continues to assess Europe's regional offer, conversations like this suggest that the next wave of partnership may emerge not from the traditional centres, but from places ready to be seen and understood on their own terms.
