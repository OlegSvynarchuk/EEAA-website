Teodor Kern, representative of the European Economic Alliance for Cooperation with Asia (EEAA), appeared on TV Pančevo, where he presented the organization's mission, activities, and long-term vision for strengthening economic cooperation between Europe and Asia.

Kern emphasized that the Association is not focused solely on cooperation between Serbia and China, but also actively works on building connections between European countries and a broader range of Asian markets. These include ASEAN countries, Central Asia, and Southeast Asia - regions representing markets of over 200 million people and offering significant growth potential for European businesses.

However, Kern underlined that successful entry into these markets requires more than economic interest. A deep understanding of cultural differences and business practices is essential. In that context, EEAA aims to bring together companies from the region, prepare them for international expansion, and provide the necessary knowledge and support to operate effectively in diverse environments.

He highlighted efficiency and a supranational character as key strengths of the Association, noting that EEAA currently brings together members from seven European countries. This structure enables a broader perspective and stronger positioning when approaching international partners.

Speaking about ongoing projects, Kern pointed to the twinning initiative between Vrnjačka Banja and Shanghai's Jing'an district, as well as cooperation with the Chamber of Commerce of Vojvodina. He also mentioned a memorandum of understanding with the Faculty of Organizational Sciences (FON) and partnerships with universities in China, aimed at strengthening academic and business ties.

Looking ahead, EEAA plans to expand its presence across both Europe and Asia within the next five to ten years, with the goal of having representatives in each country. Ultimately, the Association measures its success through the satisfaction and concrete results achieved by its member companies.

Kern concluded that now is the ideal moment for companies to enter Asian markets, as many of them are still developing. Early engagement, he noted, allows businesses to position themselves as pioneers. In this process, the unification of companies and continuous education will play a crucial role, which is why EEAA will organize workshops and host its first annual forum by the end of the year.
